# R2 — the way into Services & Pricing

**6 September 2026.** `docs/84` §2.3 named the change: not a redesign of the
tiers, *a way into them*. This is it.

---

## 1. The problem, restated from measurement

`/pricing` runs to **17.7 screenfuls on a phone**: four services, twelve
packages, twelve closed disclosures, sixteen CTAs. The tiers already match what
the field does — three levels, stacked on mobile, features collapsed, one
marked tier per service.

The defect was that four services' worth of correct tier design sat on one page
**with no door**. A buyer who came for a website scrolled past three branding
tiers and three disclosures to reach one. Measured: Marketing & Ads begins
**11.6 screenfuls** down on a phone.

---

## 2. What was built

A four-item index above the first service block, generated by
`build-pricing.js` from `pricing.json`:

> **Choose a service**
> Branding & Design — Three packages, from **490** USD One-time
> Websites — Three packages, from **250** USD One-time
> Social Media — Three packages, from **250** USD Monthly
> Marketing & Ads — Three packages, from **250** USD Monthly

Three decisions worth stating:

**It carries prices, not just names.** A chooser that only names things makes
you visit all four to compare them — which is the scroll it exists to save. The
floor and the billing period come from the same fields as the summary line
under each service block, in the same words, so the two can never disagree.

**Four anchor links, not tabs and not script.** `:target` in `01-reset.css`
already offsets for the fixed header, so this works with JavaScript off, in
both text directions, and adds nothing to the page's behaviour that can fail.

**Generated, not authored.** The service headings gained stable ids
(`#branding`, `#websites`, `#social`, `#marketing`); everything else comes from
`pricing.json`. A price edited in the data file moves the index with it.

---

## 3. Verified

Every anchor, by real fragment navigation — not a scripted click, because a
scripted click is not the thing being tested:

| | JS on | JS off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `#branding` | lands at 192px, clear of the header | same |
| `#websites` | 192px, clear | same |
| `#social` | 192px, clear | same |
| `#marketing` | 192px, clear | same |

**Eight of eight identical.** 11.6 screenfuls of scrolling replaced by one tap,
and it works with scripts disabled.

Focus follows the jump: `document.activeElement` is the target heading, so a
keyboard or screen-reader visitor arrives *at* the section rather than at the
top of a page that happens to have moved.

**Arabic, both widths:** `dir="rtl"`, arrows mirrored to the leading edge,
right-aligned, Arabic package counting correct (*ثلاث باقات، تبدأ من 490 دولار
لمرة واحدة* — the whole noun phrase, not a numeral with a plural glued on).
Link height 60px desktop, 60px phone, both clear of the 44px floor.

One test correction on the way: the first Arabic phone run reported LTR and
measured the English index while reporting a pass. The language control lives
in the drawer at that width and my click hit nothing. The harness now asserts
`dir === 'rtl'` before believing its own measurement.

`validate.js` **0** · `qa.js` **0 high, 0 medium** · `a11y.js` **0**.

## 4. The bigger finding: a broken anchor is worse than silent

Before writing a guard I checked whether one was needed — renamed a single
`href="#marketing"` to `href="#marketing-typo"` in a shipped page and ran
everything. **`validate.js` and `qa.js` both passed clean.**

Then the guard I wrote *also* missed it, and chasing that produced the real
finding.

`navigation.js` has `resolveCrossPageAnchors()`. It takes any bare fragment
whose id is **not on the current page** and rewrites it to `./#id`, on the
sound assumption that it means a homepage section — that is how the footer's
`#marketing` works from `/pricing`.

The side effect is that **a typo is laundered into a plausible cross-page
link.** The visitor taps *Marketing & Ads* in the service index and is
navigated away to the homepage. That is worse than nothing happening: nothing
happening reads as a stuck page, being sent somewhere else reads as a site that
lost your place.

It also explains why my first guard missed it. That version read the live DOM,
and by then the broken link no longer started with `#`. **Our own script had
repaired the evidence before the check arrived** — the identical blind spot
`docs/82` §5.1 found in §19, one day later, in a different file, and I walked
into it again.

`qa.js` **§22** now reads the shipped **markup**, and a fragment is sound only
if its id exists on the page **or** on the homepage, which is where
`navigation.js` will send it. Anything else is dead both ways.

Proved by breaking it:

| Mutation | |
| --- | --- |
| an index link to a missing id | **caught** |
| the skip link — `#main` → `#maim` | **caught** |

**2 of 2.** The skip link case matters: it is the first control a screen-reader
user meets on every page, and until now nothing on this site would have noticed
it pointing nowhere.

---

## 5. What this does not do

It does not shorten the page. `/pricing` is still 17.7 screenfuls for someone
who reads all of it, and that is fine — a catalogue is allowed to be long when
nobody is forced to walk it.

Whether four services should be four *pages* rather than four sections is a
different question, and it belongs with **R4** and the buyer sessions: it
changes navigation, URLs and how someone arrives from search, and it should be
decided on what buyers actually look for rather than on a scroll measurement.
