# Pixora — terms of service review

**Prepared 6 September 2026. This is the brief to send.** It replaces the two
internal working documents it was assembled from; every fact below was
re-verified against the live site on the day of writing.

---

## 0. What we are asking for

A review of one page — our terms of service, published in English and Arabic at
`/terms` — against the law of the places our clients are: **Saudi Arabia, the
UAE and Egypt.**

We have written the terms in deliberately plain language. We are not asking you
to make them sound like standard boilerplate. We are asking **whether they do
what we intend them to do**, and section 4 says what we do not need reviewed so
it is not billed for.

Sections are ordered by how much risk we think they carry. If the budget only
covers part of this, work down from section 2.

### 1.1 How a client actually reaches us — this matters more than it looks

There is **no server**. The site is static files. Nothing a visitor does sends
anything to us directly.

Every route to a conversation is one of two things:

- **WhatsApp — 27 links across the site.** The visitor's own WhatsApp opens,
  addressed to our number.
- **A contact form that is `action="mailto:"`** — it opens the visitor's own
  email client with a message drafted. If they do not then press send in their
  own mail app, **nothing exists anywhere.**

So a project begins with a written quote sent by WhatsApp or by ordinary email,
and accepted the same way. There is no signed document and no platform record —
**the record is a chat log or a mailbox.**

### 1.2 A fact we changed this week, and think you should know

**The site now composes the client's first message for them.** Every WhatsApp
link opens with text already written, for example:

> *"Hi Pixora — I'm interested in Branding & Design · Starter (from 490 USD,
> one-time)."*

The client sends that text, from their own number, without necessarily changing
a word. It names the service, the tier and a figure. We use the word **"from"**
consistently — on the button, in the message, and in the price summary beside
it — because these are starting prices, not quotes.

We flag it because it seems to us to sit close to the question in section 3.6
about invitations to treat, and we would rather raise it than have you find it.

## 2. The question we most want answered

**Nothing in the terms says which language version prevails if the two
disagree.**

Both versions are published, both are presented as *the* terms, and no clause
names one as authoritative.

We have compared them ourselves, clause by clause — **67 bilingual pairs, every
number and every clause checked. They do not currently disagree.** That is
verified, not assumed. Neither version contains a governing-language clause, and
they are symmetrical in that omission.

So the question is narrow, and entirely yours:

- **Should a governing-language clause be added, and which language should it
  name**, given the client base is largely Arabic-speaking?
- Does naming English create a problem in any of the three jurisdictions — is a
  client entitled to rely on the Arabic version regardless?

One disclosure: the Arabic has been checked for completeness and meaning but
**has not yet been reviewed by a native speaker for register and tone.** That
review is booked separately. We do not believe it affects meaning; we mention it
so you can weigh it.

## 3. The clauses that carry real risk

Each is given as **what we intend it to do**, so you can tell us whether the
wording achieves it.

### 3.1 Cancellation and the non-refundable deposit

> *"You can stop a project at any time. What has been completed up to that point
> is payable, and the opening 50% is not refundable once work has begun — it
> pays for work already done."*

**Intent:** the deposit compensates work already performed. It is not a penalty
for leaving.

- Is a non-refundable deposit enforceable in the three jurisdictions for a
  business-to-business service of this size?
- Does the answer change if the client is a sole trader or a consumer rather
  than a company?
- Would tying the retained amount to **work actually done**, rather than a flat
  50%, be safer — and is the current wording already close enough?

### 3.2 Limitation of liability

> *"what we owe you is limited to what you paid us for that piece of work. We
> are not responsible for losses beyond it — lost profit, lost data…"*

**Intent:** cap exposure at the fee for that piece of work.

- Is a cap at the fee enforceable there, and are there carve-outs that cannot be
  excluded?
- **There is no company, so this cap is the only thing between a claim and
  personal assets.** Does that change your advice on forming an entity — and is
  it a stronger reason than the tax and credibility reasons we already have?

### 3.3 Ownership transfer on final payment

> *"Everything we deliver becomes yours on completion — once the work is
> finished and paid for in full. That includes the source files. Until then it
> remains ours."*

**Intent:** the client owns the work outright once paid; retention until then is
leverage, not a permanent claim.

- Does transfer of copyright require a **signed written assignment** in the
  client's jurisdiction? If so, **can an accepted WhatsApp quote referencing
  these terms satisfy it** — see §1.1, because nothing is ever signed.
- Logos become **trademarks**. Does anything more need to happen for a client to
  register one they commissioned?

### 3.4 No promise of a business result

> *"We do not promise a business result… What we promise is the work described,
> done properly."*

**Intent:** exclude outcome guarantees while keeping a real quality obligation.

- Does this read as excluding *all* warranties, including implied ones that
  cannot be excluded? The intention is narrower than that.

### 3.5 Governing law, deliberately not fixed

> *"the law that applies is named in your written quote rather than fixed here."*

**Intent:** flexibility per client rather than imposing one forum.

- **Is deferring it a mistake?** Quotes are written by hand. If one omits it, is
  there then no agreed governing law at all?
- Would naming a default here, overridable in the quote, be better?
- Practically: is a judgment obtained in any of the three jurisdictions
  enforceable against a person resident in **Sudan** — and does that make
  arbitration, or a named seat, worth the extra words?

### 3.6 What counts as the written quote — and as acceptance

This follows directly from §1.1 and §1.2, and we think it is the question the
terms are least prepared for.

Several clauses rest on the phrase *"your written quote"*. In practice that
quote is a **WhatsApp message**, and acceptance is a reply.

- Is a WhatsApp message a *written quote* for the purposes of these terms, and
  more importantly in the law of each jurisdiction?
- Is a WhatsApp reply — *"yes, go ahead"* — acceptance sufficient to form the
  contract, and to incorporate terms published on a website that the client
  never clicked anything to accept?
- If the answer to 3.3 is that a **signed writing** is needed, does anything in
  a WhatsApp exchange satisfy it, and if not, what is the lightest thing that
  would?
- Given the record is a chat log held on personal devices: is there a
  record-keeping or evidential problem we should fix now rather than at the
  point of a dispute?

### 3.7 Published prices, and a message we wrote

- The site publishes real prices; the terms say prices may change and that a
  quote governs. **Is anything on the site an offer a client could accept
  unilaterally, rather than an invitation to treat?**
- Does the pre-filled message in §1.2 change that analysis — the client sends
  wording we authored, naming a tier and a figure? We use *"from"* throughout;
  we would like to know whether that is enough.

### 3.8 Portfolio rights

> *"We may show finished work in our portfolio — never without asking you
> first."*

- Is permission-on-asking sufficient, or is a written licence needed?
- One real client's work is already published this way, with permission given
  informally.

## 4. What does **not** need reviewing

Said plainly so it is not billed for:

- **The privacy page.** No accounts, no cookies, no server, no data collected —
  the contact form opens the visitor's own mail client and we store nothing.
  Unless you think that arrangement is itself worth a look.
- **Tone, structure and plain-English style.** Deliberate. Where a clause must
  change to be enforceable, tell us and we will change it — but a clause that is
  merely unusually plain is fine as it is.
- **The rest of the site.**

---

## 5. What we will do with the answers

Every answer becomes either a change to the terms **in both languages**, or a
recorded decision not to change it and the reason. We consider this review
finished when that is done, not when the advice arrives.

If the advice is that a company should exist, that is a larger decision and we
already have it open as the biggest unresolved question in the business.
