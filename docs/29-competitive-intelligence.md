# Phase 02 — Competitive Intelligence

WEBSTART Phase 02, run on the Phase 01 working assumptions: **A1** primary
audience H2 (established small business buying identity + site) · **A2** market
Saudi Arabia · **A3** primary goal G1 (qualified enquiries) · **A4** no client
proof is publishable · **A5** the site stays static for now.

The purpose is not to copy anyone. It is to find where the market's standard
practice is beatable, and where this project is currently outside the market
without knowing it.

---

## 0. Method, and its limits

**What was possible:** market-wide search across published Saudi and Sudanese
agency pricing, positioning and category content, plus buyer-behaviour and
channel research. Everything cited below is published material, listed in §9.

**What was not possible:** first-hand inspection of competitor websites. This
environment's egress policy blocks direct page fetches — every competitor URL
attempted returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`. So this document benchmarks **market
practice, pricing and positioning**, which are well evidenced, and marks
**UI/interaction/performance quality**, which are not, as unverified.

**Consequence:** §7 lists five pages for the owner to open and confirm. Nothing
in this analysis depends on an unverified UX claim; where a claim would need
one, it says so.

## 1. The finding that outranks everything else: the price band

Pixora's published prices are not in the same band as the market it appears to
be pricing for. This is not a small discrepancy.

| What Pixora sells | Pixora price | Published Saudi market range | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Business Website | **450 SAR** | 3,500–12,000 SAR (standard business site, 5–10 pages, bilingual) | **8×–27× below** |
| Professional Website | **700 SAR** | 8,000–20,000 SAR (mobile-first, SEO-ready, SME) | **11×–29× below** |
| Branding — Starter | **from 580 SAR** | 2,000–15,000 SAR (logo design alone) | **3×–26× below** |
| Branding — Advanced | **from 2,580 SAR** | 10,000–50,000 SAR (full brand identity) | **4×–19× below** |
| Social Media Pro | **450 SAR/mo** | 1,500–4,500 SAR/mo (published SME packages); 5,000–45,000 SAR/mo (retainers) | **3×–10× below** |
| Ads Performance | **400 SAR/mo** | same band as above | **4×–11× below** |

Nor is it a Sudan-market price list. The closest published Sudanese comparison —
Masarib Sudan — starts web design at **USD 600** (≈2,250 SAR) and sells a
site-plus-social bundle at **USD 1,200**. Pixora's flagship website is
**700 SAR ≈ USD 187**.

**So the prices sit below both candidate markets**, in the band occupied by
freelance marketplaces rather than by agencies.

### Why this is a strategy problem, not a pricing detail

1. **It silently redefines the competitor set.** At 450 SAR the real
   alternatives a buyer weighs are Khamsat/Mostaql/Fiverr sellers, not the
   agencies benchmarked here. A1's audience (an established small business)
   mostly does not shop in that band — and when it does, it is not buying a
   "one partner" relationship.
2. **The site and the price contradict each other.** The craft signals a
   considered studio; the price signals the cheapest option available. A buyer
   resolving that dissonance usually resolves it against you — *what is missing
   at this price?* Cheapness reads as risk when the purchase is a brand.
3. **The retainer thesis cannot pay for itself.** 150–450 SAR/month is below the
   cost of the work it describes (content, design, ad management) at any
   sustainable hourly rate.
4. **It makes A2 unsafe.** Phase 01 assumed Saudi Arabia because the currency
   said SAR. The price levels say something else. Until C-5 is answered, the
   competitor set below is *provisional*.

**Three readings, and only the owner can choose:** (a) the currency label is
wrong and the numbers were written for a different currency or market;
(b) ultra-budget positioning is deliberate — legitimate, but it demands a
different business model (§4.5, §5.5); (c) the list was inherited from a
freelance context and never repriced for an agency.

**This is promoted to P0.** Positioning, competitor set, proof strategy,
conversion design and KPIs all sit downstream of it.

## 3. Benchmark by dimension

| Dimension | What the market does | Where Pixora stands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pricing transparency** | Split. Many agencies quote only on request; the ones that win the "how much does X cost" search publish **ranges tied to scope**, explaining what moves a site from 8k to 20k | **Ahead on disclosure, behind on explanation.** Twelve exact prices, no ranges, no scope drivers, and "from" appears on branding only with nothing saying what it depends on |
| **Category content / SEO** | The strongest competitors rank with cost guides, comparison content and service explainers — they meet the buyer at the research moment, before the shortlist | **Absent.** Two pages, no content strategy, no organic surface. The site can only be found by people who already know it |
| **Trust and verification** | Third-party proof: Clutch and Sortlist profiles, Google reviews, and in KSA the **Maroof** badge tied to a Commercial Registration — buyers are trained to look for it | **Nothing.** No reviews, no directory presence, no CR, no Maroof, no named human, no address |
| **Proof of work** | Portfolios and case studies with outcomes; buyers open the live sites and judge speed and mobile behaviour themselves | **A Behance profile is linked but never presented as proof.** The case-study page tells the agency's own argument because no client story is permitted |
| **Conversion channel** | WhatsApp is the region's default: **92.7% usage in Saudi Arabia**, the most-used platform; response rates 5–10× email | **WhatsApp is present but passive** — a bare number, no prefilled context, competing with 21 identical form CTAs |
| **Speed to reply** | Almost nobody is fast. Average first response ≈42 hours; only 7–23% reply within five minutes, while qualification odds fall ~21× between 5 and 30 minutes | **No promise, no mechanism.** Enquiries route to a personal inbox via a mail client that may never open |
| **Scope clarity** | Weak across the band. Buyers report the decisive factor is trusting the specific people and understanding exactly what they get | **Strong.** Every package lists its features, and the six-stage process is published. This is a genuine lead |
| **Bilingual quality** | Most sites are Arabic-first or English-first with a thin counterpart | **Full parity, real RTL, no-JS switching.** Rare, and currently invisible as a selling point |
| **Performance** | Unverified for competitors (§0). Category norm is heavy WordPress builds with plugin stacks | **146KB gzipped, no framework, no dependencies** — measured. Likely a genuine advantage, and provable |
| **UI / interaction quality** | **Unverified** — requires the §7 spot-checks | Independently verified strong: no overflow, 44px targets, keyboard path, reduced-motion, AA contrast |

## 4. What they do best — five things worth taking

1. **They explain price, not just state it.** Ranges tied to scope let a buyer
   self-place before contact and pre-empt the "why so much / so little?"
   question. Pixora states twelve numbers and explains none of them.
2. **They borrow credibility from third parties.** Clutch, Sortlist, Google
   reviews and Maroof let a young agency be verified by someone other than
   itself. **None of this requires client permission** — which makes it the
   direct answer to constraint A4.
3. **They meet the buyer before the shortlist**, with cost guides and category
   content that rank for the questions buyers actually type.
4. **They put WhatsApp where the buyer already is**, rather than routing a
   regional buyer through an email form.
5. **The aspirational model productizes instead of pitching.** Designjoy's page
   answers, in public, everything a sales call would cover — price, speed,
   scope, ownership, revisions, onboarding, cancellation — and thereby removes
   the call. A solo studio holding ~USD 6k/month proves that scope clarity, not
   headcount, is what justifies a price.

## 5. Where we can be better — six openings

Each is built on something this project already has, and none requires a client
to say yes.

**O1 · Package-aware WhatsApp handoff.** The site already knows which package
moved the visitor and throws it away. A per-package WhatsApp link that opens
with *"I'm interested in Social Growth (250 SAR/month)"* converts on the
region's dominant channel, arrives pre-qualified, needs no backend, and removes
the mail-client failure mode. Nobody in this band does it well.
*Fixes: the discarded-qualification finding and half of P0-4.*

**O2 · Answer the sales call in public.** Adapt the productized principle to
this offer: delivery time per package, what "from" depends on, revision counts,
file ownership, what happens after launch, payment terms. At this price band a
buyer's biggest fear is being abandoned mid-project; the site currently says
nothing about it.

**O3 · Build proof that needs no client.** In order of cost: name the human and
show a face · a response-time promise you can keep · put the existing Behance
work on the site as work · Google Business Profile and a Clutch/Sortlist
listing · and, if the entity is Saudi, CR + Maroof. Constraint A4 removed
client proof; it never removed proof.

**O4 · Make the bilingual parity a claim, not a feature.** "Every word of this
site exists in both languages, and the Arabic is not a translation layer" is a
differentiator for any buyer serving both audiences — and it is demonstrable
in one click.

**O5 · Resolve the price band deliberately.** Either move up to the market band
with the proof from O2/O3 justifying it, or commit to the low band with a
productized model — fixed scope, a queue, no meetings, published limits — that
makes the price coherent instead of alarming. **Do not leave it undecided:**
the current state reads as an agency that does not know what it is worth.

**O6 · Let the site be the portfolio.** With no client work publishable, the
strongest evidence available is this build: measured performance, accessibility
and bilingual parity, stated as numbers a buyer can check. *"The site you are
reading is the deliverable"* is a claim competitors in this band cannot make.

## 6. Do not copy

- Hiding prices behind a quote form — the current disclosure is an advantage.
- Client logos or statistics without permission or truth. The project's content
  rule stays.
- Superlative claims ("the best agency in the Kingdom") that a young studio
  cannot support and buyers discount anyway.
- Popup and chat-widget clutter; it fights the craft the site already has.

## 8. What this changes

**New P0:** *P0-6 — decide the price band and the market together* (C-4 + C-5
answered as one decision). It gates positioning, competitor set, proof strategy
and KPIs.

**Promoted:** O1 becomes the implementation route for P0-4 (lead path) — it is
cheaper than a backend and better suited to the market.

**New P1:** third-party verification presence (Google Business Profile, a
directory listing, Behance surfaced as proof, CR/Maroof if applicable) — the
answer to the trust gap that does not wait on client permission.

**New P2:** category content — a cost guide and service explainers — as the only
realistic organic acquisition path for a two-page site.

**Assumption status:** A1 holds provisionally · **A2 is now unsafe** (the price
band contradicts the currency) · A3 holds · **A4 is weakened in a useful
direction** — proof is available without clients · A5 holds, and O1 makes
staying static viable for longer than expected.

## 11. Price re-check against 2026 published rates — 4 September 2026

**Why this section exists.** §1 benchmarked the *old* price list and produced
P0-6, which replaced it the same week. The new ladder went live today and has
never been checked against anything. A price decision that is verified once,
before it changes, is not verified.

Direct page fetches are still blocked (§0), but market search is not, so this
re-check is against **published 2026 pricing guides** — the same class of
source §1 used, refreshed. Converted at the currency pegs: SAR 3.75, AED 3.67.

### Websites — the decision worked

| Pixora, now | USD | ≈ SAR | Published Saudi range, 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Landing Page | 250 | 940 | — |
| Business Website | 650 | 2,440 | Specialist team, bilingual, 5–7 pages: **1,500–3,000** |
| Professional Website | 1,200 | 4,500 | Standard business site, 5–10 pages, bilingual: **3,500–12,000** |

**Business Website sits inside the specialist band. Professional sits inside
the standard band, in its lower third.** §1 measured the old list at 8×–27×
below; that gap is gone. This is the clearest evidence available that P0-6 did
what it was meant to do, and it is worth recording as a success rather than
only recording failures.

### Social and ads — close, still under

| Pixora, now | USD/mo | ≈ SAR | Published Saudi range, 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Social Starter | 250 | 940 | Lowest published tier seen: **1,500** |
| Social Growth | 400 | 1,500 | = that entry tier |
| **Social Pro** | 650 | **2,440** | "Basic package, content + posting": **3,000–5,000** |
| Ads Performance | 600 | 2,250 | SME management fee: **3,000–8,000** |

The **top** social tier is below the **bottom** of the basic band. Roughly
1.2×–2× under rather than §1's 3×–10×, so this moved too — but the ceiling is
still under the market floor, which means there is no tier a buyer with a real
budget can choose.

### Branding — the one that did not move far enough

| Pixora, now | USD | ≈ SAR | Published Saudi range, 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Starter | 290 | 1,090 | Logo design alone: **5,000–15,500** |
| Professional | 590 | 2,210 | — |
| **Advanced** — full identity, guidelines, system | 1,200 | **4,500** | Full brand identity: **10,000–50,000**; "medium package incl. brand guideline book": **15,000–45,000** |

**Advanced is 2.2×–11× below the full-identity band, and Starter is 4.6×–14×
below the price of a logo on its own.** Multiple independent 2026 guides agree
on that band, so this is the best-evidenced number in the section — and it is
the category where the site's own craft signal is strongest, which makes the
gap read as a claim about the work rather than about the market.

### What this does and does not mean

**It is not a call to raise prices.** `docs/31` §5 priced this deliberately as
a new entrant, remote, without the local overheads those agencies carry, and
Gate 01 froze it. Being under the band is the strategy.

**It is a call to look at one number.** Two things are true at once: websites
now land inside their band, and branding does not. If the discount is a
positioning choice it should be roughly even across the four categories, and
it is not — branding is discounted about five times harder than websites. That
is the shape of a number nobody re-derived, not the shape of a decision.

**The specific question for the owner:** *is Branding Advanced at $1,200 the
intended signal for a full identity with guidelines, when the published floor
for that deliverable in the target market is roughly triple it?* If yes, close
this. If it is a leftover from the old ladder's proportions, it is one number
in `pricing.json`, and `qa.js` will carry it to every surface on the next
build.

**Limits, stated as §0 states them.** These are published *guide* prices from
agency marketing content, which skews high — an agency publishing a pricing
guide is also selling. They are directional, not quotes. And they are Saudi and
UAE rates: Egypt, also in the target market, is materially cheaper and is not
represented here.
