# P1-2 · The CTA audit

`docs/69` P1-2. Written 5 September 2026.

Every call to action on the site, inventoried and measured — label, size,
contrast, hierarchy, repetition, and **position in the scroll** — on both
widths in both languages.

---

## 1. Method, and the correction it needed

A "CTA" here is an element carrying `.c-btn`: the site's button system. Text
links and navigation are excluded, so this measures *deliberate* calls to
action, not everything clickable.

The central measurement is **the longest stretch of scroll during which no
call to action is anywhere in the viewport.** Pages are walked one viewport at
a time first, so lazy content has settled before anything is measured.

**The first version of that measurement was wrong, and in the direction that
would have produced a false alarm.** It counted only in-flow buttons and
reported multi-thousand-pixel dead zones on desktop. The header is
`position: fixed` and **carries a live 46px CTA on desktop at every scroll
position**, so on desktop the true answer is zero. Checked before publishing,
which is the only reason this document says what it says.

The same check is what turned the finding into a real one, because on **phone
the header CTA is present in the markup at zero height** — hidden — and there
the dead zones are real.

## 2. What the inventory says, and most of it is good

Homepage, both widths:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Calls to action | **23** (22 on desktop) |
| Primary | 9 |
| Distinct labels | **21 of 23** |
| Most primaries sharing one screen | **2** — never three |
| Primary contrast | **10.89:1** — dark ink on the accent yellow |
| Controls below 44px | **0**, at any width (`docs/70`) |

Three things worth stating plainly, because they are the parts that are already
right and a redesign could easily break them:

- **The labels are specific, not generic.** Only *"Start Your Project"* repeats
  — three times on phone, four on desktop. Every other button says what it
  does for the thing it sits under: *"Ask about Starter"*, *"See what it covers
  in Branding & Design"*. That is the harder and better pattern, and it is the
  one the reference-set write-ups recommend.
- **The hierarchy holds.** Two primaries can share a screen, three never do.
  There is no screen on which the visitor is asked to choose between competing
  equal actions.
- **Contrast is 10.89:1**, against a 4.5:1 AA requirement and 7:1 AAA.

## 3. The finding: the phone has no persistent call to action, and the desktop does

This is backwards, and it is the whole of P1-2.

| | Desktop 1440 | Phone 390 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Header | `position: fixed` | `position: fixed` |
| Header CTA | **46px, visible at every scroll position** | **hidden — zero height** |
| Longest stretch with no CTA in view | **0px** | **4,660px on the homepage** |

**On desktop a visitor can act at any moment.** On phone they cannot, and the
gaps are not small:

Arabic is the same within a few percent — 4,480px on the homepage.

**Where the homepage gap falls is the part that costs money.** The 5.5 screens
run from 18,940px, and what the visitor scrolls past in them is:

> **"Everything your business needs. Connected in one system."**
> **"Need something extra? Add it when you need it."**

The integrated-offer pitch and the add-ons — the two sections written to raise
intent. A buyer persuaded by *everything connected in one system* then scrolls
five and a half screens with nothing to press.

And **/story is worse in proportion**: 80% of that page is read with no way to
act, on a page whose entire job is to make someone want to work with him.

This is the same class of defect as the eight `display: none` package buttons
found on 4 September — **a conversion path that exists on desktop and not on
the device this market actually arrives on.** That one was a CSS rule; this one
is a design decision that was never revisited after the header went fixed.

## 4. The second finding: twelve buttons open WhatsApp and none of them says so

Twelve of the 23 homepage CTAs open WhatsApp with a message pre-written. **Not
one label contains the word.** *"Ask about Starter"* gives no signal that
pressing it leaves the site and opens another app.

`docs/67` §2.4 raised this as a question for the screen-reader pass and said *"I
do not know the answer."* The inventory now gives it a size: it is not an edge
case, it is **more than half of the homepage's calls to action.**

It stays a question rather than becoming a fix here, because the answer is a
judgement about the market: in the Gulf and Egypt, WhatsApp *is* how a business
is contacted, and naming it may reassure rather than warn. **B5 and B6 are the
two people who can answer it** — `docs/68` task 1 watches exactly this moment,
and `docs/67` §2.4 asks a screen-reader user directly.

## 5. What to change, ranked

| | Change | Evidence | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Give the phone a persistent call to action.** Measured at 360/390/430: the compact header uses 139–195px of its width, leaving **~150px free between the brand and the menu trigger** — a compact CTA fits without touching either. The alternative is a sticky bottom bar, which is the stronger pattern for reach but adds chrome the site does not otherwise have | §3 | **P1-5 / X08** |
| **2** | **Put a call to action inside the ecosystem and add-ons sections** — the 5.5-screen gap is where intent is highest and reach is zero. This closes the homepage gap even if 1 is rejected | §3 | P1-5 |
| **3** | **/story needs one CTA in its middle.** 80% of the page has none | §3 | P1-5 |
| **4** | **Decide whether package buttons should name WhatsApp** — do not guess it | §4 | **B5 + B6** |
| **5** | Change nothing about labels, hierarchy or contrast | §2 | — |

**Item 1 is a visual change to the most-seen element on the site, and item 2
changes two section layouts.** Both are recommended and neither is applied
here: this document's job was to find and size them. They are P1-5's to
implement, where the whole phone experience is looked at together rather than
one control at a time.

## 6. What this does not answer

- **Whether the gap is felt.** 5.5 screens is a measurement, not an experience.
  `docs/68` task 1 — *"find the package you would choose and start a
  conversation"* — is what tells us whether a real buyer stalls there.
- **Whether the section order puts the high-intent sections in the right
  place.** That is the structural question `docs/55` §6 refused to guess and
  `docs/69` assigns to B5.
