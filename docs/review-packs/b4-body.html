1.1  How a client actually reaches us

This matters more than it looks. There is no server. The site is static files, and nothing a visitor does sends anything to us directly. Every route to a conversation is one of two things:

  • WhatsApp — 27 links across the site. The visitor's own WhatsApp opens, addressed to our number.
  • A contact form that is action="mailto:" — it opens the visitor's own email client with a message drafted. If they do not then press send in their own mail app, nothing exists anywhere.

So a project begins with a written quote sent by WhatsApp or ordinary email, and accepted the same way. There is no signed document and no platform record — the record is a chat log or a mailbox.

1.2  A fact we changed recently, and think you should know

The site now composes the client's first message for them. Every WhatsApp link opens with text already written:

“Hi Pixora — I'm interested in Branding & Design · Starter (from 490 USD, one-time).”

The client sends that text, from their own number, without necessarily changing a word. It names the service, the tier and a figure. We use the word “from” consistently — on the button, in the message and in the price summary beside it — because these are starting prices, not quotes.

We flag it because it seems to us to sit close to the invitation-to-treat question in 3.7, and we would rather raise it than have you find it.

So the question is narrow, and entirely yours:

03Read first of these

A clause that declares itself unfinished

The terms gained a section — “Promotions and reward codes” / العروض ورموز المكافآت — which opens by saying “Not final terms” and states in its own body that it has not been reviewed by a lawyer. It is published, in both languages, on a page a paying client is asked to accept.

It exists because the homepage carries a quiz that issues a discount code to whoever solves it. Its operative sentence:

“Until it is, a reward code is an invitation to talk about a discount, and the discount is agreed in your written quote like every other figure.”

Three questions, in order of how much they cost if the answer is bad:

  1. Does that sentence do what it is trying to do? The intent is that a code creates no entitlement — it starts a conversation, and the binding number is the one in the written quote. Is that how it would be read in Saudi Arabia, the UAE or Egypt, or has publishing a percentage on a public page already created an obligation regardless of what the clause says?
  2. Is “not final terms” better or worse than saying nothing? Our instinct was that flagging a draft is more honest than quietly publishing an untested clause. It is also an admission, in writing, that part of our contract is unreviewed. Tell us if that instinct is wrong.
  3. The clause lists what it will cover once written — one reward per visitor, code validity period, which packages it applies to, non-transferable, no cash value, not combinable, and our right to end a promotion. Is anything missing, and is any of it unenforceable in the three jurisdictions?

The quiz can be changed. It is ours and it is not load-bearing. We would rather change the mechanic than publish a discount we are obliged to honour on terms we did not choose. Say so plainly and we will.

04Real risk

The clauses that carry it

Each is given as what we intend it to do, so you can tell us whether the wording achieves it.

4.1  Cancellation and the non-refundable deposit

“You can stop a project at any time. What has been completed up to that point is payable, and the opening 50% is not refundable once work has begun — it pays for work already done.”

Intent: the deposit compensates work already performed. It is not a penalty for leaving.

  • Is a non-refundable deposit enforceable in the three jurisdictions for a business-to-business service of this size?
  • Does the answer change if the client is a sole trader or a consumer rather than a company?
  • Would tying the retained amount to work actually done, rather than a flat 50%, be safer — and is the current wording already close enough?

4.2  Limitation of liability

“what we owe you is limited to what you paid us for that piece of work. We are not responsible for losses beyond it — lost profit, lost data…”

Intent: cap exposure at the fee for that piece of work.

  • Is a cap at the fee enforceable there, and are there carve-outs that cannot be excluded?
  • There is no company, so this cap is the only thing between a claim and personal assets. Does that change your advice on forming an entity — and is it a stronger reason than the tax and credibility reasons we already have?

4.3  Ownership transfer on final payment

“Everything we deliver becomes yours on completion — once the work is finished and paid for in full. That includes the source files. Until then it remains ours.”

Intent: the client owns the work outright once paid; retention until then is leverage, not a permanent claim.

  • Does transfer of copyright require a signed written assignment in the client's jurisdiction? If so, can an accepted WhatsApp quote referencing these terms satisfy it — see 1.1, because nothing is ever signed.
  • Logos become trademarks. Does anything more need to happen for a client to register one they commissioned?

4.4  No promise of a business result

“We do not promise a business result… What we promise is the work described, done properly.”

Intent: exclude outcome guarantees while keeping a real quality obligation. Does this read as excluding all warranties, including implied ones that cannot be excluded? The intention is narrower than that.

4.5  Governing law, deliberately not fixed

“the law that applies is named in your written quote rather than fixed here.”

Intent: flexibility per client rather than imposing one forum.

  • Is deferring it a mistake? Quotes are written by hand. If one omits it, is there then no agreed governing law at all?
  • Would naming a default here, overridable in the quote, be better?
  • Practically: is a judgment obtained in any of the three jurisdictions enforceable against a person resident in Sudan — and does that make arbitration, or a named seat, worth the extra words?

4.6  What counts as the written quote — and as acceptance

This follows from 1.1 and 1.2, and we think it is the question the terms are least prepared for. Several clauses rest on the phrase “your written quote”. In practice that quote is a WhatsApp message, and acceptance is a reply.

  • Is a WhatsApp message a written quote for the purposes of these terms, and in the law of each jurisdiction?
  • Is a WhatsApp reply — “yes, go ahead” — acceptance sufficient to form the contract, and to incorporate terms published on a website the client never clicked anything to accept?
  • If the answer to 4.3 is that a signed writing is needed, does anything in a WhatsApp exchange satisfy it, and if not, what is the lightest thing that would?
  • Given the record is a chat log held on personal devices: is there a record-keeping or evidential problem we should fix now rather than at the point of a dispute?

4.7  Published prices, and a message we wrote

  • The site publishes real prices; the terms say prices may change and that a quote governs. Is anything on the site an offer a client could accept unilaterally, rather than an invitation to treat?
  • Does the pre-filled message in 1.2 change that analysis — the client sends wording we authored, naming a tier and a figure? We use “from” throughout; we would like to know whether that is enough.

4.8  Portfolio rights

“We may show finished work in our portfolio — never without asking you first.”

  • Is permission-on-asking sufficient, or is a written licence needed?
  • One real client's work is already published this way, with permission given informally.
05Not billable

What does not need reviewing

Said plainly so it is not billed for.

  • The privacy page. No accounts, no cookies, no server, no data collected — the contact form opens the visitor's own mail client and we store nothing. Unless you think that arrangement is itself worth a look.
  • Tone, structure and plain-English style. Deliberate. Where a clause must change to be enforceable, tell us and we will change it — but a clause that is merely unusually plain is fine as it is.
  • The rest of the site.
06Afterwards

What we will do with the answers

Every answer becomes either a change to the terms in both languages, or a recorded decision not to change it and the reason. We consider this review finished when that is done, not when the advice arrives.

If the advice is that a company should exist, that is a larger decision — and we already have it open as the biggest unresolved question in the business.
