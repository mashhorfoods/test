01Start here

Text written this week by a non-native speaker

Everything else on the site came from the client's own source material and has been in place for weeks. This section was written by us, and no Arabic speaker has ever read it. It is therefore the most likely place for something to be subtly wrong, and it is short.

1.1  Two messages the site writes for the visitor

When someone taps a WhatsApp button their message is already composed. They send it as-is. These are the words that go out under our client's own name — the first thing a client ever sends us.

WhereThe message
Any “start a project” buttonمرحبًا بيكسورا — أرغب في بدء مشروع.
A package buttonمرحبًا بيكسورا — أنا مهتم بباقة Starter ضمن الهوية والتصميم (من 490 دولار، لمرة واحدة).
  • Is أرغب في بدء مشروع how a business owner would open this, or does it read as written-for-them?
  • Is أنا مهتم بـ right, or too flat?
  • Does mixing the English package name (Starter) into an Arabic sentence read normally here? We believe it does — the client's own material does the same — but this is the place to say otherwise.

1.3  The first sentence a visitor reads

Above the fold, and where a stranger decides whether this studio is for them. The single most valuable line here to get right.

استوديو يعمل عن بُعد في الخليج ومصر. نبني العلامات والمواقع والتجارب الرقمية، ثم نربطها بالمحتوى ووسائل التواصل والإعلانات.

  1. عن بُعد — is this how a business in Riyadh or Cairo would describe a studio with no local office, or does it read as an excuse?
  2. الخليج ومصر — is naming the region this way normal, or would a buyer expect their own country named?

And beneath it, the second button: اطّلع على الأسعار

02Then this

118 strings written since the last review

Measured rather than estimated: 118 distinct Arabic strings on the homepage alone are new or changed — a six-question FAQ, a whole interactive quiz, a rewritten hero, seven photograph descriptions, a contact fallback, six region names. All written by a non-native speaker, all live, none read by an Arabic speaker. Grouped below by how much a wrong word costs.

(a)  The six FAQ questions — highest cost

These exist to answer a hesitating buyer. If one reads oddly it does the opposite of its job.

مع من سأتعامل فعليًا؟
أين مقرّكم؟
كم يستغرق المشروع؟
وماذا لو لم يعجبني التصميم؟
لمن تعود الملفات في النهاية؟
لماذا أسعاركم أقل من وكالات أعرفها؟
The one we are least sure of

The last answer argues about price, which is the easiest place to sound defensive. Read it aloud on the page and tell us whether it sounds confident or apologetic.

(b)  The Brand Challenge — about 40 strings

A quiz on the homepage: a scenario about a clinic with four complaints, four options, two attempts, then a discount code. The scenario is a paragraph of narrative Arabic — the hardest register on the site and the only place we attempt storytelling.

تأتيك عيادة بأربع شكاوى، وكلها صحيحة…

Also here: the three step labels الموجز · الإجابة · المكافأة, and المحاولات المتبقية · ليست تمامًا. · أصبتها. · صالحة لمدة 30 يومًا. · تحدَّ صديقًا

(c)  Seven photograph descriptions — heard, never seen

Each photograph carries an Arabic description a sighted visitor never sees and a blind visitor hears in full. They are long sentences:

شعار هندسي ذهبي مطبوع بارزًا على ورق داكن الملمس، تضيئه إضاءة من جانب واحد.

These do not need to be beautiful. They need to be clear and not strange. If one is over-written, say so — shorter is better here.

(d)  The contact fallback — small and load-bearing

Shown only when the visitor's email app fails to open, which means it is read by someone already slightly stuck.

إذا لم يفتح تطبيق البريد لديك، فلم يُرسَل شيء بعد. استخدم أحد هذين البديلين:

أرسلها عبر واتساب · انسخ عنوان البريد

And what happens after they make contact:

تصل رسالتك إلى مهلب، الذي ينفّذ التصميم والتطوير — لا إلى مدير حسابات.

(e)  Six names for scrollable regions — heard, never seen

A blind visitor hears these before deciding whether to enter a sideways-scrolling area:

أعمال مختارة · حملات · لوحات الهوية · شاشات الموقع · صيغ التواصل · الأعمال المسلَّمة

Each followed by — مرّر للمزيد. Is that the right instruction for something you scroll sideways rather than down? The phrase is used six times, so a better one is worth six fixes.

(f)  One clause in the Terms that says of itself it is unfinished

العروض ورموز المكافآت — ليست شروطًا نهائية.

Please read this one against the English for meaning, not register. It is a contract clause and it is also going to a lawyer, separately. If the two languages say different things, that is the finding.

03Reading order

The rest, in this order

Homepage first — it is what most people see and it carries the tone everything else follows. Then Pricing, then the case study, then About.

Terms last, and only if time remains: it has already been checked clause by clause for meaning, so it needs an ear, not an audit.

05Skip

Please don't spend the time on

  • Whether a string exists. Tooling proves it — every English string has an Arabic sibling on every page and the build fails otherwise.
  • Direction, mirroring, fonts, line breaking. Handled and verified in both directions.
  • Numerals. Western 0–9 everywhere in both languages, deliberately and enforced by the build. A page whose prices are Western-numeralled and whose body text is not asks the reader to switch systems mid-sentence. Settled.
  • The English. Deliberately plain, and not changing. If the Arabic should be warmer than the English, say so — that is the marketing-tone row above — but the English stays as it is.
06Sending it back

However is easiest

A marked printout, a voice note, or a list of phrases all work equally well. Naming the English phrase is enough — every string lives in one file with the English and Arabic side by side, so we can always find the Arabic beside it.

Each answer becomes a change in both languages, or a recorded decision not to change it and why. The review is finished when that is done, not when the notes arrive.
